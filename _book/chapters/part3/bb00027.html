BB00027: Tafsir-i-Haykali’d-Din

Part 1: “This portion relates to the commentary…”

This portion relates to the commentary on the first verse of the new ordinances which were revealed in the Haykal, but the Haykal was lost and its copy was also lost. God will surely accomplish His Cause and perfect His Light, for God has power over all things.

In the Name of God, the All-Knowing, Lord of the worlds.

Praise be to God, the All-Knowing, Lord of the worlds. Indeed, splendor proceeds from God, the One, the Primal, and from that which resembles that Oneness wherein naught can be seen save the Primal One.

I testify on this day, the first day of the responding month of the eternal year, which is mentioned as the Day of Beauty, that God, exalted be His glory, has ever existed from a beginning that had no beginning—there being no ancient before His ancientness and no first before His firstness—and will forever exist unto an end that has no end, ever ancient, there being no end to His lastness. He has existed from a beginning that had no beginning through the essence of His Being, and will exist unto an end that has no end through the essence of His eternity. His are all the most beautiful names, from their first to their last, their outward to their inward aspects, and the most exalted similitudes in their entirety, from their simple to their complex forms, their essence and their abstraction. His bounty was manifest in the beginning of beginnings and will remain the very essence of His bounty unto the end of ends.

Neither slumber nor geometry nor change nor alteration nor transformation can overtake Him. He has ever existed in the glory of His Essence, sanctified above all creation past and future, and exalted beyond all that He has brought and will bring into being. Whatsoever His creation comprehends is itself His creation by His command, and whatsoever His servants understand is His handiwork which He has originated out of nothing through His creative act. He has ever been sanctified from being associated with anything, or existing from anything, or in anything, or through anything, or upon anything, for all things have come into being through His Will, and He is their Originator through His Lordship, not from anything, but by Himself and for Himself. Whatever is recited in His praise of the Most Beautiful Names, whatever is acclaimed in His laudation of the loftiest similitudes—all this is but that every creature may know, and every servant be assured, that He is hallowed above them all and sanctified beyond their very essences. For the knowledge wherewith He is described is itself a creation which He hath created, and by it He createth; it is a knowledge He hath established through His holy Command. How, then, shall He be described by that which is His handiwork?

And the power wherewith all from the First—unto whom no first can be ascribed—to the Last—unto whom no last can be imputed—extol Him, is but a power He hath brought forth through His Will; and that Will He originated, out of no thing, by His own Self, that His creatures might declare Him far exalted above it. How, then, shall He be known by aught save Himself?

Yea, though the matter be considered in the very substance of Names or the simple realities of Attributes, even as We have made thee to know, how could He be known apart from them? I bear witness that God—exalted is He—hath ever been, and shall forever remain, free from all that He hath created and will create, lifted above all that He hath brought forth and will bring forth. All beneath Him are His creatures; whatsoever is other than He is impoverished before Him in the very fact of its existence. How, then, could the One who is the Source of being be measured by what lieth but under the shadow of its own contingent existence?

Glorified, glorified is He! Exalted, immeasurably exalted be His majesty! He shall never be beheld in His Essence, nor shall places encompass Him. Hearts cannot comprehend Him, nor can anything that exists in creation or that can perish in invention. That is but His creation in His dominion and evidence that, exalted and glorified is He, He cannot be known through it, for this has been created by His command while He is independent of His command, and how far above that which He creates and will create by His command! And whatever the signs mention in His glorification, sanctification, exaltation and unity—these are but verses created by His command in the hearts of those who prostrate, sanctify, exalt and unify, that they may be guided thereby to what is possible in contingent existence of His glorification, sanctification, exaltation and unity.

Otherwise, the glorification, sanctification, exaltation and unity worthy of the Essence cannot possibly appear in contingent existence. Indeed, none can comprehend it save the inaccessible, eternal Essence and the transcendent, most pure One. Forever has He been sanctified beyond the sanctification of what He has created and will create, glorified beyond the glorification of what He has brought into being and will bring into being, exalted beyond the praise of what He has created and will create, hallowed beyond the unity of what He has created and will create.

Glory be unto Thee, O God! Neither I nor anything else, from a beginning that had no beginning, has known Thee, nor can I or anything else know Thee, unto an end that has no end. Glory be unto Thee! I am among those who recognize that the knowledge of God, exalted and glorified is He, is impossible in its primary essence—how much more so in what has perished or will perish through it—by its testimony in the Book. I bear witness to this from a beginning that has no beginning throughout all its manifestations, and am certain of it unto an end that has no end throughout all its concealments. And since knowledge is impossible, how can creation find a way to anything less? For other conditions come after the perishing of knowledge and its realization.

Exalted is God far above this with great exaltation, and glorified is God beyond this with mighty glorification! When thou art certain of what I have taught thee of the dawning-places of the mention of the First and the daysprings of the breezes of the morn of eternity, then bear witness that everything wherewith God thy Lord is described—that is a description God has brought into being, and everything denied of God of attributes unworthy to be mentioned of God—these are brought into being by what God has brought into being. God has forever been unknown through either of these, for both were created in His dominion by His command.

Lo! The guides of insight have been permitted by God to describe Him with these, and He loves not that creation describe Him with other than these. Yet this is from the limitations of creation, not from what is worthy of the inaccessible, transcendent Essence. Otherwise, were either of these closer to His Essence, then remoteness from the other would necessarily be attributed. Glory be unto Him, glory be unto Him, beyond all that He has created and will create! Let all prostrate to Him as far as possible in creation, let all sanctify Him as far as possible in invention, let all exalt Him as far as possible in origination, let all unify Him as far as possible in bringing into being. When He created the beginning that had no beginning, this was like His creation of what He will create unto the end that has no end—all are in His grasp that He may be remote from what is possible therein. He is independent of them and of what is possible therein, while they are in need of Him, glorifying His praise, sanctifying through His tribute, exalting through His sanctification, and unifying through His exaltation.

Glory be unto Him, glory be unto Him! Know that creation hath no beginning and no end, lest thou shouldst imagine a cessation in God’s bounty or a pause in His creation. The First, which hath no beginning in contingent existence, was a potential wherein God created all things through His Primal Will, and fashioned a single temple in the image of man. And all that is possible unto the Last that hath no end shall be created after the pattern of what was created, and let not thy knowledge be bounded by thinking that God’s power could be elevated above something and not descend upon it, for this is impossible with God, the Self-Sufficient, the Powerful. Rather, all that can be manifested from God hath been manifested and will be manifested. Therefore be thou tranquil, content, detached and assured, for whatsoever can be brought into being by God shall be brought into being, and none can prevent it, and God is powerful over it and knoweth it.

When thou hast understood this, thou wilt be certain that creation hath no beginning whereby thou mightest reckon from its inception, and preserve this as one of the bounds of thy religion. In every Revelation all things are renewed and created anew. This is the beginning of the reckoning, for the First that hath no beginning hath ended at the Primal Point in every Revelation, ending until it ended at Muhammad. And what thou hast seen in the reckoning of the second after the tenth, and what hath passed of the years from the manifestation of the Primal Point to the manifestation of the Bayan—this is of the bounds of that kingdom.

God desireth not that anyone should preserve the reckoning of that kingdom save from Manifestation to Manifestation, even as thou thyself had no knowledge before the appearance of the Primal Point, nor knowledge of its reckoning. Likewise from after the appearance of the Primal Point to the Point of the Bayan. Therefore bear witness to this and make the beginning of thy reckoning from the manifestation of the Point of the Bayan, and limit not the duration of that creation by a fixed bound of twelve thousand years or a thousand or above that or below that, but rather reckon it from the First that hath no first, for this is more steadfast before God’s power and more beloved before God and before those who are endowed with knowledge from Him.

When thou hast understood this, bear witness that God hath created the creation of all that is in the Bayan through its Point, and this is the meaning of the true saying “God created all things through the Primal Will and created the Primal Will by itself,” for this is manifest where God revealed Himself to the Point of the Bayan through itself, by itself, of itself, and caused it to speak verses which are its proof from its Lord apart from aught else. Then He created through it all who are in the Bayan. Thus in every Revelation the creation of all things is renewed, therefore ascend at every Revelation.

When thou hast understood this and art assured of it, then look upon the Temple of Religion [Haykali’d-Din]. The first that God revealed in that Religion by His own tongue, which all His creation is powerless to match, is this holy verse:

“God beareth witness that there is no God but Him, the King, Possessor of the kingdoms.”

In the First Gate He hath ordained mention of His Self that there is no God but Him, to Him belong the most excellent names. All that are in the heavens and the earth and what lieth between them glorify Him, and all in the kingdoms of Command and Creation and what lieth beneath them prostrate before Him—all are His servants and all bow down before Him. Verily, were I to desire to expound, in the tongue of knowledge and wisdom, that which God hath sent down in the tongue of His very Identity, the oceans of all that He hath created and will yet create would be powerless to set forth even a single letter thereof. For the station of every utterance is according to the capacity of its speaker. Who can measure the power of God, that he may know the measure of His Word? And who can ascend to the throne of God’s Essence, that he may comprehend the station of His Word?

Glorified, glorified be He with the most exalted glorification! I testify that God’s Word is His utterance, and God’s utterance is His Command, and all things stand through His Command. Behold then the loftiness of His Word, how it is the cause of the creation of all that hath been and shall be created, and the sustenance of all things. Even as thou art certain that there is no God but Him in essence, know thou that His Word hath no equal, and therefore all else is powerless before it. For His Word, glorified be His name, hath lordship and dominion over all utterance. Therefore do the mountains of essence bow down and humble themselves at the mention of God and His Word.

Whoso heareth the Word of God and is not enkindled with the love of God in his heart and soul, how can he be considered alive in the life of hearts? Thus doth the sincere believer, upon hearing the Word of God, turn instinctively toward God and humble himself at His mention. This is among the signs of the dwellers of the paradise of life, the eternal garden of delight. And whoso possesseth not this glorious attribute is not counted, in the sight of God and those endowed with knowledge, among the inhabitants of the paradise of truth.

Therefore did Ali, in the days of the Qur’an, upon hearing the Surih “Read!” from Muhammad, the Messenger of God, become wholly attracted unto Him, beholding naught else but His Cause. Likewise in the Bayan, when the first to believe in the Point of the Bayan heard the verses of the First Book, he was wholly attracted and humbled himself at the mention of his Lord’s name. Such hath ever been God’s way from a beginning that hath no beginning to an end that hath no end. Know then, even as thou knowest, the true reality of the Word of God; for, by God! it is more precious, more exalted, more inviolable, more uplifted, more auspicious, more holy, and more sublime than that the essences of thought among the men of insight should encompass its measure, or that the most hidden subtleties of perception, though borne by the keepers of lamps and of vision, should attain unto it. And just as thou hast believed that none can grasp His inmost Essence nor know Him as becometh His station, so likewise recognize the Word of God and its rank; for all that He hath created and will create are powerless to comprehend its worth or to reach the summit of its estate.

Be thou assured that by it is the whole of religion brought into being—from the first mention of the Primal Will unto the ultimate mention of the atom. This is the very essence of causes and the lamps of majesty, in the sanctity of pre-eternity and the might of consummation. Wherefore, if thou apprehendest aught—according to what is possible within the realm of possibility—of the praise due to the Word of God and of its station, then deem that verse most abundant in bounty, and call to witness the emergence of unalloyed knowledge and the very quintessence of power. For in that single verse hath God—glorified and exalted be He—sent down all the concerns of religion, from its beginning to its end, from its outward form to its inward truth.

Oh, how I yearn to behold those who shall appear at the close of the Manifestation of the Bayan, and who shall draw forth from that verse the lamps of knowledge and exposition, in a manner newly-arisen therein: the inner recesses of thought shall be quickened, and the simple gazes ennobled. And all this shall be according to the measure of their being, the bounds of their knowledge, and the limits of their deeds. Otherwise, the Word of God is assuredly worthy of what is higher, more exalted, more inviolable, more sublime, and more hallowed than that it should be comprehended by any save God—or by the Sun of His Manifestation—or by whomsoever He willeth among the dawnings of His knowledge and the pure essences of His Cause.

Therefore behold with thine inmost being the creation of that most impregnable Sign, wherein God hath testified concerning Himself, not that which His creation hath testified. Had He not sent the Messengers and revealed the Books, would aught have diminished from His dominion? And after sending them, was aught added unto His sovereignty?

Rather, He created the Sun of Truth solely through His bounty and pure generosity, that it might derive strength from God its Creator and glory from God its Originator. When He created it through the Tongue of God by itself—for example, when God revealed to Muhammad, the Messenger of God, that there is no God but Him—He manifested unto him that he should speak from God, saying:

“Verily We have sent thee as a witness, a bearer of glad tidings, a warner, one who summoneth unto God by His leave, and as a luminous lamp.”

Through this Word was the Sun of Truth created, such that from the beginning that hath no beginning it hath been the Throne of God’s manifestation, and unto the end that hath no end it shall be the Seat of God’s revelation.

Thus have I shown thee how that Reality is wrought by Himself for His own Self—albeit He hath no “how”; for whatsoever possesseth a “how” must needs be fashioned by the Will. And verily, the being of the Divine Will is the Sun of Reality: its hidden mystery is the ocean of the Names and Attributes, and its outward splendour the Kingdom of Command and Manifestations. Naught descendeth from God save through it, and naught ascendeth unto God, forevermore, save by it. This is the Cause of causes—pre-existent and everlasting—from the First of the first unto the Last of the last, unceasing, unaltered.

Ponder its sign in the sun of the firmament: though God cause it to rise without end, it is but one sun; and though He cause it to set without end, it remaineth the one sun. Every Name of goodness is mentioned beneath its shadow and created for its sake; and whatsoever is beneath it of praiseworthy mention is brought forth by it.

Yet be not veiled from its inmost self, neither ascribe unto it what is unbefitting. In its descriptions it is remembered as the Sun of Reality, the Dawning of Pre-Eternity, the Orientation of all orientations, the Onticity of Everlastingness, the Ipseity of Absolute Oneness. And in certain invocations and portrayals it is commemorated as the Essence of God and His Ipseity, the Self of God and His He-ness, the Face of God and His Countenance, the Hearing of God and the Eye of His might, the Tongue of God and the Hands of His power—and the like of such evocative expressions and veiled allusions. All these are tokens whereby proof is established that dominion taketh its rise from the King and returneth unto the King.

These, one and all, are honorific ascriptions, even as God aforetime ascribed to Himself the clay of the Sacred House and named it the House of His Essence—though exalted is He above the ascriptions of the creatures, whether to their pearl or to their mote, and sanctified from the confines of place, be it the loftiest height or the lowliest depth. Yet through this connection He createth the reality of that House and distinguisheth it. Similarly, through such transcendent attributes and exalted descriptions, He createth the Sun of Truth and distinguisheth it through its relationship to God, its Lord. For God is exalted above being known through any but Himself or being described by other than His Own Self—glorified, glorified be He with sublime glorification.

God hath mentioned in the Second Gate concerning the faith of all in each Dispensation in the Manifestation of His Self. Were anyone to contemplate this religion, he would surely witness every religion that God hath created and will create from a beginning that hath no beginning unto an end that hath no end. Behold then the all-encompassing nature of this verse and the extension of God’s power therein, where God, glorified be He, hath through His utterance made manifest all who have appeared from a beginning that hath no beginning, and hath revealed all in the Point of the Bayan. Then the mention extendeth to Him Whom God shall make manifest, referring thereby to Him Whom God shall make manifest, and what is mentioned between them is the creation of the Bayan, as it is created through the Will. In this verse there is no station for what is created by the Will; rather, it is the station of its mention and its abstraction, its transcendence and uniqueness from all else. Verily, He Who hath appeared is identical with Him Who shall appear, and He Who shall appear is identical with He Who hath appeared, just as the sun that rose yesterday is identical with the sun that shall rise tomorrow.

Therefore, all the Prophets from a beginning that hath no beginning find their existence in Him Whom God shall make manifest, and all who shall appear unto an end that hath no end derive their reality from Him Whom God shall make manifest. Thus in the ocean of names, if thou hast comprehended and aided it, thou shalt see naught but God, for these are mirrors wherein naught is seen save God. And if thou comprehendest in this verse the station of its sun-like nature, it is as though thou hast comprehended all the Prophets who have appeared in existence and all who shall appear in possibility, for what is manifest in all the Prophets is one single manifestation from God, glorified be He.

God hath testified to the servitude of Who hath appeared and Who shall appear as His servant, and the verse hath been made unique in mentioning this uniqueness, as it speaketh not of them as His servants and His proof, for both are one single soul. God hath distinguished among all created attributes that of servitude, for pure servitude hath appeared therein and not in aught else, and if it appeareth in others it is like the sun in mirrors which cannot be compared to the sun in the heavens. The ʿAyn is His knowledge of God, the Ba is His glory from God, and the Dal is His perpetuity from the Essence of God, for this reality is the essence of the glory of God after His knowledge and before His perpetuity.

Then God distinguished after the mention of His servitude the mention of His being the Proof, wherein the Ha is life, the Jim is bounty, and the Ta is the addressing “Thou.” God hath explained through the mention of the Proof every good name that hath been and shall be created in contingent existence, and the meaning of the Proof is the one who exerciseth authority by God’s authority over all things, for God, glorified be He, hath bestowed upon the Sun of Truth that through Him and through it His authority is manifested over all His creation, and there is no distinction between Him and God—His Manifestation—save that He is His servant, His Proof, His tokens, His testimony, His testimonies, and His Balance. By this verse are the people of the Bayan distinguished from those that came before; for in every Dispensation the word hath been: “There is none other God but God.”

Yet in every Dispensation, the Cause is made manifest through that second verse which is the station of the mention of the First, the Light shining forth from the Morn of Eternity and the Glory that has ever dawned. Then observe in all the world what God hath explained in His saying “and all are created by God’s command from Him.” For He created His own Self, as hath been made clear to thee that it is from God, exalted be He, by Himself and for Himself, not through any intermediary other than Himself. And after Himself, all are created from Him—meaning that through what He uttereth from God’s presence, as He willeth, from the potentiality of possibility to the atom of existence.

For all the ways of religion have appeared from Him, and were He not to explain, nothing would appear, and were He not to will to explain a command or prohibition, that thing would not be created. Thus all the paths of religion are from Him for the establishment of that verse, and that verse is for the establishment of the first verse in the ocean of names. And that verse is the station of its outward manifestation while the first verse is the station of its inward reality wherein naught is seen save God, its Lord. And God hath mentioned the mention of all things so that nothing might escape from being an effect in relation to the causation of the Will, from the highest pearl to the lowest atom. This is the station of God’s power over all things through His essential knowledge and the dominion of God’s proof over all things through His essential substance.

Therefore observe that God createth all things through the Will, and that all besides God are His creation, created by His deed. And the first mention of the deed is in the appearance of the Will unto whatsoever God willeth to manifest. And it hath ended in that Manifestation with the One Knowledge, the One Lamp which ought neither to increase nor decrease. And even if all creation in the world of names would suffice unto God through the first verse, yet since creation existeth within the bounds of creatureliness, God hath therefore sent down another verse for the exaltation, independence, inaccessibility, radiance, glory and might of the first verse, and such like lofty attributes and exalted descriptions. By Him Who created Paradise and seeth the breath of life, Who is single in might and unique in grandeur and exalted in loftiness—wert thou to witness all upon earth traversing the ocean of names, each one would make thee the first verse and thou wouldst be content therewith apart from aught else, for that verse of Him Whom God shall make manifest sufficeth His own Self above all souls while no soul sufficeth apart from His Self.

But when thou didst witness the dwellers of that most glorious horizon to be rarer than red sulphur and scarcer than the appearance of the adorned essence which was created in its essential reality without any maker fashioning it, thou wert content with the first verse for all creation in their knowledge, deeds, origin and source. Yet when thou didst witness the powerlessness of all before that, the other verse was sent down for those in the ocean of creation. Then if thou hadst witnessed all on earth believing in it and walking in God and in the Manifestation of His Self, no third verse would have been sent down after it, and it would have sufficed, for with God this is the same as with the Manifestation of His Self.

Yet when I beheld that the people were voyaging upon the oceans of limitations and veils, and that their hearts found no tranquility in the oneness of God nor in faith in the Manifestation of His own Self, nor tasted delight in that divine Garden and heavenly Paradise—I thereupon expounded the first verse by means of the second, and then the second by the second, even as God willed, until the number of the One was perfected by the One, and the number of Baha was consummated within the One—this, that creation might not be drawn into the depths of their own affairs but rather return to their Source and Ultimate End. All verses that have been revealed in every Dispensation after the Two Verses are for their exaltation, majesty, invincibility, sublimity, and glory, and whatsoever lofty attributes may be mentioned in their praise and whatsoever exalted descriptions may be offered in their honor. And thus it continues endlessly—all that appears in that religion is for the establishment of the Second Verse in that Gate, which in turn is for the establishment of the First Verse, for God, the Protector, the Exalted.

When it has been established through divine verses, heavenly manifestations, eternal revelations, signs of unity, and expressions of oneness that first comes the word “There is no God but God” followed by mention of the Manifestation of God’s Revelation, these Two Witnesses are the essence and foundation of religion. Were God to give ownership of all that is on earth to anyone, He would not accept from them save these Two Words. Therefore, observe their glory, though this is only to the extent possible in the contingent realm. Otherwise, were God to transmute all that is possible in the realm of contingency into the red elixir, He would not accept from anyone an alternative to the Other Word that “Ali ibn Abdullah is His servant and proof.”

I seek God’s forgiveness for such limitation, for the glory of that Word with God is greater, but this is only according to the measure of what is possible in contingency, where I have drawn this metaphor. Otherwise, God, my God and the God of all things, bears witness that all things were created through it. How then could the glory of the effect be equated with the Cause? Exalted is God far above this with supreme exaltation, and glorified is He above this with transcendent glorification.
