BB00151: Tafsir-i-Hadith-i-Haqiqat (Commentary on the Tradition of Truth)

Part 1: “One day Kumayl ibn Ziyad al-Nakha’i(Following…”

One day Kumayl ibn Ziyad al-Nakha’i1 rode behind Ali2 (peace be upon him) on his camel.

Kumayl asked, “O my master, what is Truth?”3 Ali replied, “What have you to do with Truth?”4 Kumayl said, “Am I not a confidant of your secret?”5

Ali answered, “Indeed you are, but that which overflows from me only trickles upon you6.” Kumayl said, “Would one such as you disappoint a seeker?”7 ʿAli (peace be upon him) replied, “The removal of the veils of glory without indication.”8

Know that His word encompasses all things9 and flows through all worlds, for speech is a manifestation among the manifestations10 of the Speaker. God, exalted be He, made it the manifestation of His encompassing nature^[On “Word” as locus of divine disclosure: compare the Shi’i identification of the Imam as “kalimat Allah” and “hujja” (e.g., al-Saduq, al-Tawhid; al-Kafi), and the broader Qurʾanic motif of “His Word” (kalimatuhu).

The Arabic: ja’alahu mazhar ihatatihi wa-tajallihi.] and His revelation, and of all His appearances, for the Essence alone encompasses itself11, and He is the Encompasser and nothing encompasses Him. The station of conjunction12 is the station of His action and His manifestation, which is a creation created by its Maker through Himself^[“Created by its Maker through Himself” recalls Shi’i reports on creation “by the will” (bi’l-mashiʾa) and the first creation as a mediating reality (al-Kafi on mashiʾa; al-Saduq).

In Sufi terms, it parallels emanation through the divine command (kun) and the first determination (ta’ayyun awwal).] and established in His shadow13. All expressions in the station of knowledge express His station, and the encompassing nature of His word is the encompassing nature of the Word of God—nothing escapes from beneath His shadow14.

When you understand this introduction, know that Kumayl’s state is revealed through his answer15—that he was not perfect in the station of servitude16, else he would not have asked about Truth^[The critique of “asking about al-haqiqa” echoes the Hadith of Kumayl’s own structure (questioning the haqiqa and receiving graded answers).

In Sufi epistemology, the Real (al-Haqq) is not an object of discursive inquiry but of dhawq/mushahada; cf. al-Hujwiri and Ibn Arabi on the self-evidence of the Real.], for that which is asked about is Truth itself, and it is not other than itself17. Rather, it is more evident than to be asked about18, as the Prince of Martyrs^[“Prince of Martyrs” (Sayyid al-Shuhada) refers to Imam Husayn.

The citation is from DuaArafah, a foundational Shii devotional text with strong theophanic language, widely commented upon in later Shii irfan* and also appreciated in Sufi circles for its metaphysics of presence.] said in the prayer of ʿArafah: “Can anything other than You have a manifestation19 that You lack, so that it would be that which manifests You? When wert Thou ever hidden, that Thou shouldst need an indication to lead to Thee?

When wert Thou ever distant, that traces should lead to Thee? Blind is the eye that sees Thee not20 ever watching over it! What a losing bargain he has made who has not taken a share of Thy love!”

Had Kumayl not seen himself as riding alongside him21, he would not have ventured such speech with him. Had he known himself, he would not have presumed to ride alongside him, for his reality is but a trickle22 of what overflows from His glory.

How can a ray ride alongside the orb of the sun?23 That is impossible, for a thing cannot surpass its source24. Kumayl erred when he saw himself riding alongside him and imagined his own self and reality, so he asked about Truth^[“Truth” translates al-Ḥaq={i}qa, a key term in Sufi triads (shar={i}a/ṭar={i}qa/ḥaq={i}qa).

In Shii usage here it is tied to Imam Ali’s esoteric authority: inquiry into ḥaq={i}qa requires annihilation of self-claim (daw={a}).]. He (peace be upon him) said “What have you to do with Truth?”25 meaning that you are in the station of duality26, and that is association more distant than the two Easts, while He is closer to you than your jugular vein27—what have you to do with Truth?

When the servant heard and recognized the association within himself28, he feared before his Creator and his dark self became subtle. He said, “Am I not a confidant of your secret?”29

He (peace be upon him) said “Indeed,” showing kindness so as not to extinguish the fire of his love30, and that which overflows trickled upon him. He made known to him through his words “that which overflows from me only trickles upon you”31 that your reality, O Kumayl, is a trickle from the station of the Prophets^[“Station of the Prophets” evokes the hierarchy of maqamat: prophetic reality as the source of saintly/initiatic shares.

Sufi literature often speaks of inheriting from the prophets (waratha) and of the Muhammadan Reality. Shi’i sources similarly present the Imams as inheritors of prophetic knowledge and light, with companions receiving only partial shares.], and they are what overflows from me. In other words, the station of your reality about which you ask is a trickle of what overflows from his reality. When Kumayl heard the servant’s question, he established himself in the station of servitude32 and became humble before his master, saying “Would one such as you disappoint a seeker?”

Then the veils were rent and he became capable of beholding the lights of the beauty of His reality, which was His manifestation of it through it at the beginning of its existence through His words “O fire, be coolness and peace!”33 He said: “The removal of the veils of glory without indication,” O Kumayl, so remove all veils for God created them. Their Creator is established in the ocean of glory without indication to me or to thee, for indications are among the veils, and veils are pure coverings and absolute obscurity.

They represent the station of names and attributes, while glory is the station of the Named One and the negation of attributes manifests His divinity34. Know that Truth is eternal and contingent beings are created. Truth is too exalted to descend to the realm of possibility, and possibility is prevented from ascending to eternity.

Therefore, divine wisdom requires that the Eternal Truth describe Himself to creation35 so that creation may know its Creator and contingent beings may reach their ultimate purpose through His eternal outpouring. This description is created36, incomparable to any description, and an identity to which nothing can be likened.

This description is the reality of the servant—whoever knows it knows his Lord37, as indicated by the Imam: I knew Thee through Thee38, Thou guided me to Thee and called me to Thee. Were it not for Thee, I would not have known what Thou art.’ And he said: Know God through God^[“Know God through God” (i’rifu’Llah bi’Llah) resonates with Shi’i epistemology: God is known by His own self-disclosure, not by analogy.

Compare apophatic passages in Nahj al-Balagha and al-Saduq’s al-Tawhid. Sufi analogues include the principle that ma’rifa is a divine gift, not acquired by discursive proof alone.].’ This is the description expressed in some stations as the heart39, and this description is the Lordship which is the essence of servitude and the sign which God showed in the horizons and within souls40 until it becomes clear to creation that He is the Truth.

Look with the eye of thy heart: thy reality is the Lordship of thy Lord for thee through thee—thou art He and He is thou, except that thou art thou and He is He. He has a station of unity, His identity is pure essence41—no mention, no indication, no expression of this station except through inability, and this is the perfection of unity through negation of attributes and Lordship, which when there is no object of Lordship has no mention, no encompassing, no manifestation. Through this awareness He knew Himself, detached from names, attributes and deeds, and through this awareness is unveiled through reasoning the knowledge of names, attributes and deeds from God, glorified be He.

Part 2: “Verily, God, exalted be He, is manifested unto…”

Verily, God, exalted be He, is manifested unto thee through thee42, observeth thee through thee, and encompasseth thee through thee43. This is thy highest Paradise^[“Highest Paradise” corresponds to the Arabic “jannatuka al-a’la,” evoking Qurʾanic al-Firdaws/al-Illiyyun and the Sufi interiorization of paradise as a state of proximity (qurb) rather than a locale.

Shi’i devotional texts likewise speak of the “paradise” of ma’rifa (gnosis) and wilaya.] and thine utmost sanctuary—the Furthest Mosque44—for the denizens of the Garden of Ridvan45 possess naught save the Remembrance of God, the Most Great46, and the Name of God, the Most Mighty, the Most Bountiful.

This station may not be indicated by indication47: in the perfection of its nearness it is far, and in the perfection of its farness it is near. No veils conceal it. He is above all things—hidden by the mystery, veiled by the secret, secreted within the secret—and naught availeth for the knowledge of Him but the secret48.

Unto this point alludeth the report from Ali—upon him be peace—addressed to Kumayl49: “A soul of the kingdom (nafs malakutiyyah)50, endowed with a divine potency (quwwah lahutiyyah)^[”Lahut” (divinity) as a level beyond malakut is widely used in Sufi and Shi’ite gnosis to denote the divine aspect manifest in the Perfect Human.

Compare the Sufi schema of lahut/jabarut/malakut/nasut and Shi’ite discussions of the Imam’s “divine” authority (wilayah) as a theophanic potency.], a simple essence, living by its very self; its root is the Intellect (al-aql)51. From the Dawn of Pre-Eternity52 it took its beginning, by it it summoneth, unto it it pointeth, and unto it doth it return when perfected and made like unto its source.

From it have beings come forth, and unto it do they return in consummation.” [Report attributed to Ali for Kumayl] It is the Most Exalted Self of God, the Tree of Tuba53, the Lote-Tree of the Extremity (Sidrat al-Muntaha)54, and the Garden of Refuge (Jannat al-Maʾwa).

Whoso knoweth it shall never be wretched; whoso is ignorant of it shall stray and be undone. He that attaineth unto Majesty shall not be miserable; but he that is drowned in the Ocean of the Effulgences (as-Subuhat)55 and thereby veiled from the encounter with the Reality, that one wandereth and is astray.

These intimations pertain to the unveiling of the “Effulgences,”56 and these indications are for the attainment unto “Majesty.”57 When, therefore, the lights of Beauty are unveiled^[“Lights of Beauty” (anwar al-jamal) evokes tajalli al-jamal in Sufi manuals: theophany as light unveiling.

In Shi’i theosophy, recognition (ma’rifa) is tied to divine Names and Attributes; the Bab frames self-unveiling as the condition for ma’rifat al-Rabb, resonant with the maxim “who knows himself knows his Lord.”] from thyself, thou wilt recognize thy Lord, Possessor of Majesty and Bounty.58 Another meaning: the unveiling of the Effulgences is the entering of the City—Majesty—59 without “indication,”^[Arabic: min ghayr ishara—without allusive pointing.

In Sufi epistemology, ishara marks symbolic teaching for novices, while the realized know by dhawq (tasting) and iyan (direct seeing). The Bab’s “without indication” implies immediacy: jalal is accessed when signs fall away.] that is, when its people are heedless.60 Another meaning: thy very reality is Majesty—it is the Face of thy Lord61—but not a “facedness” that is pointed to.

In sum: Kumayl could not bear the state62. Either he rose and sought a further epiphany63, or he was confounded^[“Confounded” corresponds to hayra (perplexity), a classic station in Sufi manuals (maqam al-hayra) and in Akbarian thought as the apex of knowing God through the collapse of conceptual determination.

Shi’i mystical commentaries likewise treat hayra as the mind’s arrest before the Imam’s unveiled realities and the divine Names.] and knew not how to speak. He said, “Increase me in exposition64.” He replied: “The effacement of the imagined (mahw al-mawhum)65—that is, the effulgences^[”Effulgences” glosses subuhat (often: luminous splendors/veils).

In Sufi literature, subuhat can denote dazzling lights that both reveal and veil, recalling the hadith of God’s “veil of light” (hijab al-nur). Shi’i gnostic texts likewise speak of luminous veils surrounding the divine presence and the Imam’s light (nur).]—and the soberness of the known (sahw al-ma’lum)66—that is, Majesty^[“Majesty” renders jalal, commonly paired with jamal (beauty) in Sufi theologies of the Names. The passage’s identification of sahw with jalal suggests a transition from luminous “splendors” to awe-inspiring divine rigor.

Wherefore Kumayl, after explanation and after saying “Increase me,”67 found it not and attained not that which he sought. He said, “Increase me in exposition.” He replied: “To rend the veil68 through the ascendancy of the secret.”
